# Provenance of the paper's tables and figures

Audit of `STAR_GNN_BB_v2.tex` (the version with Nithin's comments). For each
reported number: the script that produced it, the raw output it can be
recomputed from, and whether it was reproduced.

All regenerated results use the shared failure definition in
`gnn_pipeline/decoding_failure.py` (a shot fails if the estimate does not
reproduce the syndrome, or reproduces it and flips a logical; X and Z
components combined by OR). Every regenerated experiment uses fresh data with
its own RNG seed and stores per-shot failure vectors, so every LER and every
McNemar count can be recomputed from raw outcomes.

Status key: **traced** = script and stored output in the repo;
**regenerated** = no original source, re-run with a committed script;
**untraceable** = no script or raw output anywhere in the repo or its history.

## Details

### Table 2: hyperparameters
Table 2 lists hidden dimension 64, GRU updates, lr 1e-4, batch 16, cosine
schedule with 2-epoch warmup, syndrome-loss weight 0.1, 154k/183k parameters.
These are the defaults of `gnn_pipeline/train_supervised.py`; the runs
trained with them (`runs/`) are git-ignored and not in the repo.
The headline result (Table 4) was produced by `audit_confound1.py` with a
different configuration: hidden dimension 32, 3 layers, **39,329 parameters**,
AdamW lr 2e-3, weight decay 1e-4, batch 64, 20 epochs, constant learning
rate, focal loss (alpha 0.25, gamma 2) only, no syndrome loss, 10-iteration
flooding BP (scaling 0.8) for both training and evaluation. The capacity test
(`audit_confound4.py`) used hidden 32, **2** layers, lr 3e-3, batch 32,
50 epochs. Table 2 must either describe the model the results come from or
be split per experiment.

### Invariance checks (Sec. "Uniform-prior scaling invariance")
Regenerated counts of shots with bit-identical hard decisions (2,000 shots each):

| Decoder | 288, p 0.015 vs 0.0286 | 72, p 0.04 vs 0.02 |
|---|---|---|
| ldpc min-sum, serial, 100 iter | 2000/2000 | 2000/2000 |
| ldpc min-sum, flooding, 100 iter | 2000/2000 | 2000/2000 |
| repo `MinSumBPDecoder` (flooding, 10 iter) | **1982/2000** | 2000/2000 |
| ldpc sum-product, serial, 100 iter | **1975/2000** | **1957/2000** |

- The repo's own decoder clamps messages to +/-20 (`clamp_llr`), which breaks
  exact scaling on the 288 code. The paper's 1740/1740 claim "in the
  implemented decoder" does not hold for this decoder.
- Sum-product is not invariant, as the proposition predicts. The paper's
  statement that the 1,000-shot test was identical for sum-product is not
  reproduced.
- The eta sweep (eta in {1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100}, p = 0.03, 72 code, 5,000
  shots) is reproduced: identical decisions and 174 failures at every eta.

### Table 4: headline result
Traced to `audit_confound1.py`; the epoch-5 numbers reproduce bit-exactly.
Problems: all data files use seed 42, so test shots share random streams
with training shots; training p actually spans [0.005, 0.045], not
[0.02, 0.03]; the checkpoint was selected on the test set. Replaced by the
leakage-free five-seed re-run (`audit_headline_v2.py`, `results/headline_v2/`):
12.5% +/- 0.6% at p = 0.04, 10.6% +/- 0.7% at p = 0.06.

### Capacity test
Rerun under the corrected failure definition (same data: first 1,000 shots
of `data/big72_test_p04.npz`, trained and evaluated on the same shots):
BP 105 failures, GNN 93 at the best epoch (5): **11.4%**, n10/n01 = 15/3,
McNemar p = 0.0095. Paper: 14%, 17/3, p = 0.004.

### Table 5 / Fig. F4: GNN + OSD vs serial BP-OSD, [[72,12,6]]
Regenerated with the five headline_v2 checkpoints on fresh data, 20,000 shots
per p. Serial min-sum BP-OSD (scaling 0.8, 100 iterations, OSD-CS-10). The GNN
corrections are added to the prior LLR log((1-p)/p) of both components, as in
the headline evaluation.

| p | BP-OSD | GNN+OSD, 5 seeds | Seeds significantly worse (p < 0.05) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 0.0093 | 0.0095-0.0102 | 1/5 |
| 0.03 | 0.0297 | 0.0306-0.0317 | 5/5 |
| 0.04 | 0.0676 | 0.0688-0.0709 | 4/5 |
| 0.05 | 0.1354 | 0.1384-0.1418 | 5/5 |

The paper's conclusion holds: the GNN never helps BP-OSD and is significantly
worse from p = 0.03. The absolute BP-OSD values differ from the paper
(0.0046 / 0.0377 / 0.0831 / 0.1515), in inconsistent directions; without the
original script this cannot be explained.

### Table 6 / Fig. F2: code-capacity oracle gap
Regenerated: per-qubit rates drawn fresh per shot, serial min-sum BP-OSD-CS-10
with a uniform prior vs the true per-qubit rates, 50,000 shots per cell.

The paper's text says p_i ~ LogNormal(log p_mean, sigma), i.e. p_mean is the
median. Its mean-prior error counts barely change with sigma, which only
happens if the **mean** rate is held fixed. The mean-anchored run (median
p * exp(-sigma^2/2)) reproduces the paper:

| Code, p | sigma = 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.0 | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 72, 0.04 | 10.8% (3508) | 28.7% (3432) | 65.1% (3438) | 11 / 30 / 66% (3457, 3420, 2988) |
| 144, 0.06 | 12.0% (3165) | 34.8% (3203) | 79.9% (2936) | 16 / 38 / 74% (276, 268, 2604) |
| 288, 0.07 | 12.8% (3338) | 40.9% (3307) | 88.3% (2786) | 18 / 48 / 87% (922, 524, 1750) |
| 144, 0.04 | 19.8% (324) | 45.5% (275) | 85.3% (278) | not in paper |
| 288, 0.04 | 14% (21, n.s.) | 18% (17, n.s.) | 88% (17) | not in paper |

Parentheses: mean-prior failures. All cells at the paper's operating points
have McNemar p < 1e-20. Notes:
- The paper's 144 row has 276 and 268 mean-prior errors at sigma = 0.25 and 0.5
  but 2,604 at sigma = 1.0. Those counts match p = 0.04 for the first two cells
  (324, 275 here) and p = 0.06 for the third, so the row appears to mix
  operating points. The 288 row's counts (922, 524, 1750) match no single
  operating point either.
- A uniform prior at the mean and at the median give identical decisions
  (min-sum scaling invariance), so the mean-vs-median question about the prior
  is moot; only the noise normalisation matters.
- The median-anchored variant (`results/tables_v2/oracle.json`) gives
  similar gaps (10-66% at the paper points) but at a much higher total noise
  for sigma = 1.

### Table 7 / Fig. F3 and the rate-estimating GNN
No code in the repo implements per-qubit OU drift with a W = 32 window, the
"Empir" frequency estimator, or a GNN that estimates per-qubit rates. Fig. F3
hard-codes the table values. These results cannot be checked and must be
re-run from the original code or removed.

### Table 8 / Fig. F6: circuit level
Regenerated in step 3 (`audit_circuit_v2.py`, `results/circuit_v2/`). The
original circuits lacked first-round and final detectors; the "no oracle
gap" result was an artifact. With complete detectors the oracle gap is 56% at
p_mean = 0.006 (McNemar p = 1.5e-23).

- Every code-capacity file in `data/` was generated with seed 42.
